## Activities

### 1. Evaluate Exit Criteria

**Exit Criteria Framework**:

**Retention Metrics**:

**What to Measure**:
- **Day 7 Retention**: % of users active 7 days after signup
- **Day 30 Retention**: % of users active 30 days after signup
- **Monthly Active Users (MAU)**: Users active in last 30 days
- **Repeat Usage**: % of users using product repeatedly

**Thresholds** (examples, adjust for your product):
- **Day 7 Retention**: 40%+ (good), 60%+ (excellent)
- **Day 30 Retention**: 20%+ (good), 40%+ (excellent)
- **Repeat Usage**: 50%+ (good), 70%+ (excellent)

**Why Retention Matters**:
- Retention indicates product value
- High retention = users finding value
- Low retention = product not meeting needs
- Retention is leading indicator of product-market fit

**Paid User Rates**:

**What to Measure**:
- **Conversion to Paid**: % of users converting to paid
- **Paid User Growth**: Growth rate of paid users
- **Revenue per User**: Average revenue per paid user
- **Lifetime Value (LTV)**: Estimated lifetime value of paid user

**Thresholds** (examples, adjust for your product):
- **Conversion to Paid**: 10%+ (good), 30%+ (excellent)
- **Paid User Growth**: 10%+ monthly (good), 20%+ monthly (excellent)
- **Revenue per User**: Varies by pricing model

**Why Paid User Rates Matter**:
- Paid users validate willingness to pay
- High conversion = strong product-market fit
- Low conversion = value proposition needs work
- Paid users are leading indicator of business viability

**Product-Market Fit Signals**:

**What to Measure**:
- **Repeatable Acquisition**: Can you acquire users consistently?
- **Repeatable Sales**: Can you convert users to paid consistently?
- **ICP Validation**: Is your ICP clearly validated?
- **Channel Validation**: Do you have validated acquisition channels?
- **Product Validation**: Does product deliver value consistently?

**PMF Indicators**:
- **Strong Signals**: 
  - Repeatable acquisition and sales for given ICP
  - High retention and paid conversion
  - Clear ICP validation
  - Validated acquisition channels
  - Product delivering consistent value
- **Weak Signals**:
  - Inconsistent acquisition
  - Low retention and conversion
  - Unclear ICP
  - No validated channels
  - Product not delivering consistent value

**Document in Swift CNS**:
- Create exit criteria evaluation
- Document retention metrics
- Document paid user rates
- Document PMF signals
- Create PMF evidence document

### 2. Assess Product-Market Fit

**Product-Market Fit Definition**:

**PMF = Repeatable Acquisition and Sales for Given ICP**

**What This Means**:
- You can acquire users consistently (not just one-time)
- You can convert users to paid consistently (not just one-time)
- This works for a specific ICP (not all users)
- This is repeatable (not luck)

**PMF Assessment Framework**:

**Acquisition Repeatability**:
- Can you acquire users consistently?
- Do you have validated acquisition channels?
- Is acquisition predictable?
- Example: LinkedIn ads consistently drive 20 signups/week

**Sales Repeatability**:
- Can you convert users to paid consistently?
- Is conversion predictable?
- Do you have validated sales process?
- Example: 30% of signups convert to paid within 30 days

**ICP Validation**:
- Is your ICP clearly validated?
- Do you have evidence ICP works?
- Is ICP consistent across experiments?
- Example: Engineering Managers at 50-200 employee companies consistently work

**Product Value Delivery**:
- Does product deliver value consistently?
- Do users find value repeatedly?
- Is value delivery predictable?
- Example: 60% of users complete first action, 40% return for second

**PMF Score** (self-assessment):
- **Strong PMF**: All indicators strong, ready for public beta
- **Moderate PMF**: Most indicators strong, may need more validation
- **Weak PMF**: Few indicators strong, need more work

**Document in Swift CNS**:
- Create PMF assessment document
- Document acquisition repeatability
- Document sales repeatability
- Document ICP validation
- Document product value delivery
- Create PMF evidence summary

### 3. Plan Transition to Public Beta

**Transition Planning**:

**From Private Beta (Controlled Waitlist)**:
- **Current State**: Controlled access, waitlist, manual approval
- **Characteristics**: Limited users, high touch, controlled growth
- **Benefits**: Quality control, focused learning, manageable support

**To Public Beta (Immediate Signups)**:
- **Target State**: Immediate access, no waitlist, automated signup
- **Characteristics**: Open access, self-service, scalable growth
- **Benefits**: Scale, growth, market validation

**Transition Strategy**:

**Quota-Based Approach** (Recommended):
- **Phase 1**: Small quota (e.g., 50 signups/week)
- **Phase 2**: Increase quota (e.g., 100 signups/week)
- **Phase 3**: Increase quota (e.g., 200 signups/week)
- **Phase 4**: Remove quota (full public beta)

**Why Quota-Based**:
- Controls growth rate
- Manages operations costs
- Allows gradual scaling
- Reduces risk

**Operations Cost Alignment**:

**Cost Considerations**:
- **Infrastructure**: Server costs scale with users
- **Support**: Support costs scale with users
- **Operations**: Operations costs scale with users
- **Team**: Team capacity limits growth

**Cost Alignment Strategy**:
- **Calculate Cost per User**: Infrastructure + support + operations
- **Set Quota Based on Budget**: How many users can you support?
- **Scale Quota with Revenue**: As revenue grows, increase quota
- **Monitor Costs**: Track costs as you scale

**Example Cost Alignment**:
- Cost per user: $5/month (infrastructure + support)
- Budget: $1,000/month
- Quota: 200 users/month (within budget)
- As revenue grows, increase quota

**Transition Timeline**:

**Week 1-2**: Evaluate exit criteria, assess PMF
**Week 3**: Plan transition, set quota, prepare systems
**Week 4**: Execute transition, monitor closely
**Ongoing**: Scale quota based on performance and costs

**Document in Swift CNS**:
