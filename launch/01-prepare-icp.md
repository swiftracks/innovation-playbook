# 01 — Prepare ICP

**Purpose**: Develop comprehensive B2B ICP framework before private beta launch

**Outcome**: Have a detailed B2B ICP framework (account-level and role-level) documented as testable hypotheses in Swift CNS, ready to validate through private beta experiments

**Audience**: PM / Dev / Both

**Time**: 2-4 hours

**Prerequisites**: [Decide What to Build](decide/index.md) - Initial ICP research, [Launch & Iterate](index.md)

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
1. Develop B2B ICP framework with account-level attributes (Industry, Company Size)
2. Define role-level ICP attributes (Role, Pains, Gains, Life Without Solution)
3. Leverage earlier research from the decide section to inform ICP
4. Conduct competitor analysis to identify market gaps and warm channels
5. Document ICP as testable hypotheses in Swift CNS
6. Frame ICP for private beta success and validation

## Jobs-to-Be-Done

- **When**: I have initial ICP research from the decide section and am preparing for private beta
- **I want**: To develop a comprehensive B2B ICP framework
- **So that**: I can validate the right ICP through private beta experiments and ensure they find the platform and stay

## Inputs

- Initial ICP research from [Decide What to Build](decide/index.md)
- User research on pains, gains, and life without solution
- Competitor research and market analysis
- Understanding of target market from earlier validation
- Swift CNS account for documenting ICP hypotheses

## Activities

### 1. Review Earlier Research

**Leverage Decide Section Research**:
1. Review problem statements and user research from [01 — Idea / Problem](decide/01-idea-problem.md)
2. Review assumptions and hypotheses from [02 — Identify Critical Assumptions](decide/02-identify-critical-assumptions.md) and [03 — Form Testable Hypotheses](decide/03-form-testable-hypotheses.md)
3. Review experiment results and learnings from [04 — Design & Run Experiments](decide/04-design-run-experiments.md) and [05 — Extract Key Learnings](decide/05-extract-key-learnings.md)
4. Extract key insights about who your users are and what they need

**What to Extract**:
- Who experiences the problem?
- What are their characteristics?
- What pains do they have?
- What gains do they seek?
- What does their life look like without your solution?

### 2. Develop Account-Level ICP

**B2B ICP Framework - Account Level**:

**Industry**:
- Which industries have this problem?
- Which industries are most likely to pay?
- Which industries have budget for solutions?
- Example: SaaS companies, Professional Services, Healthcare Tech

**Company Size**:
- What company size (employee count) is ideal?
- What company size has budget?
- What company size has the problem at scale?
- Example: 50-500 employees, $10M-$100M revenue

**Account Characteristics**:
- What other account attributes matter?
- Technology stack?
- Growth stage?
- Geographic location?

**Document in Swift CNS**:
- Create ICP hypotheses as testable statements
- Example: "We believe Engineering teams at SaaS companies (50-500 employees) will use our retrospective tool"

### 3. Develop Role-Level ICP

**B2B ICP Framework - Role Level**:

**Role**:
- What specific role experiences the problem?
- Who has budget authority?
- Who influences purchase decisions?
- Example: Engineering Manager, VP of Engineering, Product Manager

**Pains**:
- What specific pains does this role experience?
- What keeps them up at night?
- What frustrates them daily?
- Example: "Wasted time in retrospectives", "No actionable insights", "Repeating same issues"

**Gains**:
- What gains does this role seek?
- What outcomes do they want?
- What would make them successful?
- Example: "Actionable insights", "Faster retrospectives", "Better team outcomes"

**Life Without Solution**:
- What does their current process look like?
- What tools do they use today?
- What workarounds do they have?
- What's the cost of the status quo?
- Example: "Manual retrospective analysis", "Spreadsheets", "No clear action items", "2 hours wasted per sprint"

**Document in Swift CNS**:
- Create role-level hypotheses
- Link to account-level ICP
- Frame as testable statements for private beta

### 4. Conduct Competitor Analysis

**Competitor Research**:

**Identify Competitors**:
- Direct competitors (solve same problem)
- Indirect competitors (alternative solutions)
- Adjacent solutions (related problems)

**Analyze Competitor GTM**:
- Where do they market? (channels)
- Who do they target? (ICP)
- What messaging do they use?
- What pricing do they offer?
- What's their positioning?

**Identify Market Gaps**:
- What ICP segments are underserved?
- What channels are underutilized?
- What messaging gaps exist?
- What pricing opportunities exist?

**Identify Warm Channels**:
- Where are competitors active?
- What communities do they participate in?
- What events do they attend?
- What content do they create?
- These are "warm channels" you can participate in

**Document in Swift CNS**:
- Create competitor analysis Learning Card
- Document market gaps as opportunities
- Document warm channels for channel strategy

### 5. Validate ICP Through Experiments (Not Assumptions)

**ICP as Hypotheses**:
- ICP is NOT assumed—it's validated through experiments
- Before validation, ICP is a "prospect" (as per three-phase model)
- Private beta will validate ICP through Explore → Validate → Scale phases

**Frame ICP for Private Beta**:
- What ICP signals will you look for?
- How will you know if ICP is correct?
- What experiments will validate ICP?
- What metrics indicate ICP fit?

**Example ICP Hypothesis**:
```
We believe Engineering Managers at SaaS companies (50-500 employees) 
will find our retrospective tool valuable and become paying customers.

We'll know this is true when:
- 60%+ sign up from this ICP segment
- 50%+ complete first retrospective
- 40%+ return for second retrospective
- 30%+ convert to paid

If these metrics aren't met, we'll explore other ICP segments.
```

**Document in Swift CNS**:
- Create ICP hypotheses as experiments
- Define success criteria
- Plan validation experiments

## Worked Example

**Situation**: Developing ICP for retrospective tool

**Earlier Research** (from decide section):
- Problem: Engineering teams waste time in retrospectives
- Users: Engineering teams of 5-15 people
- Pains: No actionable insights, repeating issues
- Gains: Faster retrospectives, better outcomes

**Account-Level ICP**:
- Industry: SaaS companies, Professional Services, Healthcare Tech
- Company Size: 50-500 employees, $10M-$100M revenue
- Characteristics: Tech-forward, growth stage, US/Europe

**Role-Level ICP**:
- Role: Engineering Manager
- Pains:
  - Wasted time in retrospectives (2 hours per sprint)
  - No actionable insights from feedback
  - Repeating same issues sprint over sprint
  - Manual analysis is time-consuming
- Gains:
  - Actionable insights automatically
  - Faster retrospectives (30 min vs 60 min)
  - Clear action items for next sprint
  - Better team outcomes
- Life Without Solution:
  - Current: Manual retrospective analysis, spreadsheets, no clear action items
  - Tools: Google Docs, Miro, Jira
  - Workarounds: Manual pattern recognition, team discussions
  - Cost: 2 hours wasted per sprint, repeating issues

**Competitor Analysis**:
- Competitors: Retrium, Parabol, EasyRetro
- Their ICP: Similar (Engineering teams)
- Their Channels: LinkedIn, Engineering communities, conferences
- Market Gaps: Smaller teams (5-10 people), AI-powered insights
- Warm Channels: Engineering communities (r/agile, Dev.to), LinkedIn groups

**ICP Hypothesis in Swift CNS**:
```
We believe Engineering Managers at SaaS companies (50-500 employees) 
will find our AI-powered retrospective tool valuable.

We'll know this is true when:
- 60%+ sign up from this ICP segment
- 50%+ complete first retrospective
- 40%+ return for second retrospective
- 30%+ convert to paid

Validation experiments:
- Explore: Test messaging to different ICP segments (2 days)
- Validate: Test with target ICP segment (1 week)
- Scale: Scale winning ICP segment (2 weeks)
```

**Result**: Clear ICP framework ready for private beta validation

## Extended Worked Example: FlowAgent AI

**Product Context**: FlowAgent AI is an AI agent that proactively manages cross-functional workflows by monitoring Slack/email/project tools, identifying bottlenecks automatically, and taking autonomous action to unblock teams.

**Why 2025 AI-Specific**: Requires LLM capabilities for context understanding, agentic behavior (proactive vs reactive), learning company workflows, generating contextual communications, and autonomous decision-making.

**Multi-Vertical**: Works for Product, Sales Ops, Customer Success, Engineering, Marketing—any team with cross-functional dependencies.

### Earlier Research (From Decide Section)

**Problem Identified**:
- Cross-functional teams lose 8-12 hours/week to workflow bottlenecks
- Manual follow-ups consume 30% of manager time
- Critical blockers go unnoticed for days
- Context switching kills productivity

**Users**: Operations Managers, Product Managers, Project Managers at companies with cross-functional teams

**Pains**: Manual status tracking, missed dependencies, delayed escalations, too many tools

**Gains**: Automated workflow management, proactive problem-solving, reduced context switching

### Account-Level ICP

**Industry** (Multi-Vertical Approach):
- **Primary**: SaaS companies (Product/Engineering teams)
  - Why: High complexity, cross-functional dependencies, tech-forward
- **Secondary**: Professional Services (Client delivery teams)
  - Why: Project-based workflows, multiple stakeholders
- **Tertiary**: Tech-enabled companies (Operations teams)
  - Why: Growing operations complexity, need automation

**Company Size**:
- **Sweet Spot**: 50-200 employees
  - Why: Large enough to have workflow complexity, small enough to need efficiency
  - Pain is acute: Growing fast, processes breaking, can't hire fast enough
- **Secondary**: 200-500 employees
  - Why: Established processes, budget available, scaling challenges

**Account Characteristics**:
- **Technology Stack**: Uses Slack + project tools (Linear, Jira, Asana)
- **Growth Stage**: Growth-stage (Series A/B), scaling rapidly
- **Geography**: North America (US/Canada), English-speaking
- **Culture**: Data-driven, adopts new tools quickly

### Role-Level ICP

**Primary Role**: Head of Operations / VP Operations
- **Why**: Owns cross-functional workflows, has budget authority, feels pain acutely
- **Budget Authority**: Yes (tools budget $50K-$200K/year)
- **Decision Making**: Primary decision-maker, influences stakeholders
- **Problem Ownership**: High (directly responsible for workflow efficiency)

**Secondary Roles**: 
- Product Managers (influence, experience pain)
- Engineering Managers (influence, experience pain)
- Chief of Staff (decision-maker in smaller companies)

### Pains (Head of Operations)

**Pain 1**: "I spend 10 hours/week manually tracking workflow status"
- **Frequency**: Daily (2 hours/day checking Slack, tools, following up)
- **Severity**: 8/10 (extremely frustrating, takes time from strategic work)
- **Impact**: Can't scale operations team, burnout risk, missing strategic initiatives
- **Quote**: "I'm a human workflow router. It shouldn't be my job."

**Pain 2**: "Critical blockers go unnoticed for days"
- **Frequency**: Weekly (2-3 major blockers slip through per week)
- **Severity**: 9/10 (causes project delays, missed deadlines, frustrated teams)
- **Impact**: Product delays, customer impact, team morale issues
- **Quote**: "By the time I notice the problem, we've already lost 3 days."

**Pain 3**: "Too many tools, context switching kills productivity"
- **Frequency**: Daily (switching between Slack, Jira, Linear, Asana, email 50+ times/day)
- **Severity**: 7/10 (exhausting, increases error rate)
- **Impact**: Mental fatigue, mistakes, decreased work quality
- **Quote**: "I have 12 tabs open just to see what's blocked."

**Pain 4**: "No visibility into cross-functional dependencies"
- **Frequency**: Daily (dependencies across Product, Eng, Design, Sales)
- **Severity**: 8/10 (causes coordination failures)
- **Impact**: Delays, rework, finger-pointing
- **Quote**: "We only discover dependencies when they're already broken."

**Pain 5**: "Reactive, not proactive problem-solving"
- **Frequency**: Constantly (always firefighting, never preventing)
- **Severity**: 9/10 (strategic vs tactical trap)
- **Impact**: Can't focus on process improvements, team growth limited
- **Quote**: "I'm always two steps behind. I want to be two steps ahead."

### Gains (Head of Operations)

**Gain 1**: "Automated workflow monitoring without manual effort"
- **Importance**: High (eliminates 10 hours/week of manual work)
- **Outcome**: Time freed for strategic work, scalable operations

**Gain 2**: "Proactive identification of blockers before they cause delays"
- **Importance**: High (prevents 2-3 major delays per week)
- **Outcome**: Faster delivery, happier teams, better customer outcomes

**Gain 3**: "Single pane of glass for all workflows"
- **Importance**: Medium-High (reduces cognitive load)
- **Outcome**: Faster decision-making, reduced errors, less burnout

**Gain 4**: "Autonomous action on routine blockers"
- **Importance**: High (AI agent takes action without human intervention)
- **Outcome**: Truly automated operations, 10x scalability

**Gain 5**: "Data on workflow patterns to drive process improvements"
- **Importance**: Medium (enables strategic optimization)
- **Outcome**: Continuous improvement, measurable efficiency gains

### Life Without Solution (Head of Operations)

**Current Process**:
1. Start day with 30 min "Slack catch-up" (scroll through 15+ channels)
2. Open Jira, Linear, Asana to check status (15 min)
3. Identify 5-7 potential blockers manually
4. Send follow-up messages to 8-10 people (30 min)
5. Context switch to other work
6. Repeat 3-4x throughout day (2 hours total)
7. End day with "status sync" email (20 min)

**Current Tools**:
- **Slack**: Communication, but no workflow intelligence
- **Linear/Jira/Asana**: Project tracking, but siloed, no cross-tool visibility
- **Email**: Follow-ups, but manual and reactive
- **Spreadsheets**: Manual workflow tracking (nightmare to maintain)
- **Loom**: Recording status updates (time-consuming)

**Workarounds**:
- **Daily standup meetings**: 30 min/day with 10 people = 5 hours team time/day
- **Status update Slack channels**: Manual updates, inconsistent
- **"Check-in" culture**: Constant interruptions, reduced deep work
- **Personal tracking**: Each manager has their own system (chaos)

**Cost of Status Quo**:
- **Time**: 10 hours/week per Ops person (100 hours/week for 10-person ops team)
- **Money**: At $100K salary = $50K/year in wasted time per person
- **Opportunity Cost**: Strategic projects delayed, can't scale team, losing competitive edge
- **Frustration**: High burnout, turnover risk (recruiting cost $50K-$100K per person)
- **Total Cost**: $500K+/year for 10-person operations team

### Competitor Analysis

**Direct Competitors**:

**Asana/Monday/ClickUp (Project Management)**:
- **ICP**: All teams, broad market
- **Channels**: Google ads, content marketing, product-led growth
- **Messaging**: "Organize work", "Collaborate", "Stay on track"
- **Positioning**: Collaboration platform
- **Gap**: Not AI-powered, not proactive, requires manual updates

**Motion/Reclaim (AI Scheduling)**:
- **ICP**: Individual contributors, managers
- **Channels**: LinkedIn, Product Hunt, influencer marketing
- **Messaging**: "AI-powered calendar", "Focus time"
- **Positioning**: Personal productivity
- **Gap**: Focuses on individual scheduling, not cross-functional workflows

**Indirect Competitors**:

**Zapier/Make (Workflow Automation)**:
- **ICP**: Ops teams, technical users
- **Channels**: Content marketing, integrations directory
- **Messaging**: "Automate workflows", "Connect apps"
- **Positioning**: Integration platform
- **Gap**: Requires manual workflow building, not intelligent/adaptive

### Market Gaps Identified

**Gap 1**: AI-powered, proactive workflow management (not reactive project tracking)
- **Opportunity**: No competitor offers true agentic behavior for cross-functional workflows
- **Positioning**: "The AI agent that manages workflows proactively, not a tool you have to manage"

**Gap 2**: Multi-tool intelligence (works across Slack, project tools, email)
- **Opportunity**: Competitors are siloed within their tools
- **Positioning**: "One AI agent across all your tools"

**Gap 3**: Operations-focused ICP (not broad "all teams")
- **Opportunity**: Competitors target broad market, leaving operations teams underserved
- **Positioning**: "Built for Operations teams at growth-stage companies"

**Gap 4**: Autonomous action (not just notifications)
- **Opportunity**: Competitors notify but don't act
- **Positioning**: "AI agent that actually unblocks workflows, not just alerts you"

### Warm Channels Identified

**Communities**:
- **r/ops** (Reddit): 50K+ operations professionals
- **Product Hunt**: New AI tools, tech-forward early adopters
- **RevGenius** (Slack): 50K+ operations/revenue professionals
- **Operations Nation** (community): Operations leaders

**Events**:
- **SaaStr**: Operations track, 10K+ attendees
- **Operations Summit**: Ops-focused, 2K+ attendees
- **SaaS Growth Summit**: Growth-stage companies

**Content Channels**:
- **Operations blogs**: "How we scaled operations at [Company]"
- **LinkedIn**: Operations leaders sharing challenges
- **Podcasts**: Operations podcasts (20+ focused on ops)

**Competitor Channels** (Warm):
- LinkedIn (all competitors active)
- Product Hunt (new tool launches)
- Integration directories (Slack App Directory, Zapier)
- G2/Capterra (review sites)

### ICP Hypothesis for FlowAgent AI

```
We believe Heads of Operations at SaaS companies (50-200 employees) 
will find FlowAgent AI valuable for proactively managing cross-functional 
workflows and become paying customers.

We'll know this is true when:
- 50%+ sign up from this ICP segment (Ops leaders at SaaS 50-200)
- 60%+ connect Slack + project tool within 24 hours (activation)
- 50%+ have AI agent identify 3+ blockers in first week (value delivered)
- 40%+ return weekly for 4+ weeks (retention)
- 30%+ convert to paid within 60 days (paid conversion)

If these metrics aren't met, we'll explore:
- Alternative ICP 1: Product Managers at SaaS companies (100-500 employees)
- Alternative ICP 2: Engineering Managers at tech companies (200-500 employees)
- Alternative ICP 3: Chiefs of Staff at growth-stage companies (50-200 employees)
```

### Validation Plan for Private Beta

**Explore Phase (Days 1-4)**:
- **Experiment**: Outreach to 3 ICP segments (Ops, PM, EM) via LinkedIn
- **Sample**: 10 prospects per segment (30 total)
- **Signal**: Which segment responds fastest? Which has highest meeting conversion?
- **Decision**: After 4 days, focus on segment with 40%+ meeting conversion

**Validate Phase (Weeks 1-2)**:
- **Experiment**: Private beta with 15 users from winning segment
- **Metrics**: Activation rate, blockers identified, retention
- **Pattern**: Do 50%+ activate and find value in first week?
- **Decision**: After Week 2, validate ICP or pivot to alternative

**Scale Phase (Weeks 3-8)**:
- **Experiment**: Scale to 50 users from validated ICP
- **Metrics**: Retention, paid conversion, referrals
- **Validation**: Do 40%+ retain weekly? Do 30%+ convert to paid?
- **Decision**: After Week 8, confirm PMF for ICP or iterate
