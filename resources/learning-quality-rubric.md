# Learning Quality Rubric

Use this rubric to assess whether learning outputs are decision-ready.

The purpose of a rubric is not to make teams score things for the sake of scoring. It is to give teams a shared way to judge whether a learning is strong enough to support synthesis and decision-making.

## Scoring model

Rate each dimension from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong):

- **Evidence strength**: data quality and relevance.
- **Traceability**: link from learning to hypothesis and assumption.
- **Clarity**: unambiguous statement of what changed.
- **Confidence**: explicit confidence and uncertainty.
- **Decision relevance**: usefulness for go/iterate/pivot/stop decision.

## What the dimensions mean in practice

### Evidence strength

- `1-2`: evidence is thin, indirect, or hard to trust.
- `3`: evidence is useful but still limited.
- `4-5`: evidence is clear, relevant, and strong enough to materially affect confidence.

### Traceability

- `1-2`: learning is loosely connected to the cycle.
- `3`: traceability exists but is incomplete.
- `4-5`: learning clearly links back to the experiment, hypothesis, and original assumption.

### Clarity

- `1-2`: wording is vague or overly interpretive.
- `3`: the point is understandable but still imprecise.
- `4-5`: anyone reviewing the artifact can clearly understand what changed.

### Confidence

- `1-2`: confidence is missing or overstated.
- `3`: confidence is present but not well justified.
- `4-5`: confidence and uncertainty are both visible and proportionate to the evidence.

### Decision relevance

- `1-2`: interesting, but unlikely to change a decision.
- `3`: somewhat useful, but still indirect.
- `4-5`: directly helps the team decide whether to go, iterate, pivot, or stop.

## Interpretation

- 20-25: decision-ready.
- 14-19: usable with targeted improvements.
- 5-13: not decision-ready; revise before synthesis.

## How to use this well

- Score collaboratively when possible.
- Use low scores to identify exactly what needs work.
- Do not average away obvious weaknesses. One critical weak dimension can still make the learning hard to use.
- Treat the rubric as a coaching tool, not a compliance ritual.

## Common mistake

Teams often give high scores to learnings they *want* to be strong. The best protection against that is to ask: would a skeptical but fair reviewer agree with this score based on the evidence provided?
