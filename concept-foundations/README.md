# Concept Foundations

**Purpose**: Establish a common language and operating model before execution.

## Outcome

Teams can run faster cycles with fewer misunderstandings and stronger decisions.

By the end of this section, teams should not just know the terminology. They should understand why the terminology exists, how the stages connect, and how stronger concepts lead to stronger execution.

## Time to complete

45-60 minutes.

## Read in order

1. [Core Definitions](core-definitions.md)
2. [Learning System Overview](learning-system-overview.md)
3. [Decision Quality Model](decision-quality-model.md)

## Why this matters

Most execution delays do not begin in the experiment itself. They begin earlier, when teams:

- use the same words to mean different things,
- move between stages without clear quality bars,
- synthesize too early,
- or make decisions without shared confidence in the evidence.

This section exists to standardize the mental model before the work gets more expensive.

## What this section is really doing

The purpose of concept foundations is not to slow teams down with theory. It is to remove avoidable ambiguity so the rest of the workflow moves faster.

Done well, this section helps teams:

- reduce debate caused by terminology confusion,
- improve the quality of handoffs between roles,
- increase consistency across projects and cohorts,
- recognize weak reasoning earlier in the cycle,
- make later decisions with stronger confidence and less rework.

## What good looks like

A team that completes this section well can:

- explain the difference between each stage of the loop,
- identify what kind of artifact they are producing at any given step,
- evaluate work quality before it becomes a downstream problem,
- align founders, operators, reviewers, and mentors around the same standards.

## Definition of done

- Team uses shared terminology consistently.
- Team understands the cycle model and artifact flow.
- Team can apply decision-quality standards during reviews.

## Common failure mode if skipped

When teams skip this layer, they often mistake movement for progress. Work gets created, but the team cannot easily tell whether it produced evidence, learning, insight, or just more discussion.

That is why this section comes before the operational workflow pages.
