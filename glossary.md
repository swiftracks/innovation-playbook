# Glossary

Use this page to keep the SwiftCNS language consistent across the playbook. These definitions are meant to improve shared judgment, not just vocabulary.

## Core Terms

### Idea or Problem
The starting point for a learning cycle: a situation, opportunity, or challenge worth understanding. In SwiftCNS, ideas and problems matter only when they are translated into explicit assumptions that can be tested.

### Bet
The broader initiative, product direction, or strategic move the team is trying to make work. Assumptions, experiments, learnings, and decisions should all stay connected to the bet they are meant to strengthen or challenge.

### Assumption
A belief that must be true for a bet, initiative, or product direction to succeed. Assumptions are where uncertainty begins. They are not facts until they have been tested well enough to change confidence.

### Critical Assumption
An assumption that, if proven false, would invalidate the core value proposition or make the solution non-viable. Critical assumptions are high-risk and should be tested early.

### Hypothesis
A testable statement derived from one assumption. A hypothesis turns belief into something measurable so the team can define what signal would support or weaken it.

### Experiment
A structured test designed to validate or invalidate a specific hypothesis. A real experiment is not just activity. It is activity designed to generate evidence that informs a decision.

### Activity
Work that is happening, but is not necessarily learning-focused. An activity becomes an experiment only when it has a clear hypothesis, defined signal, and direct relevance to a decision.

### Observation
Something noticed during research, delivery, or experiment execution. An observation is not yet a learning until the team can responsibly interpret what the evidence supports.

### Evidence
The data, signals, behaviors, or results produced by an experiment or other structured input. Evidence is the raw material that supports learnings and should be strong enough to review, not just discuss.

### Learning
An evidence-backed conclusion produced from experiment results. A learning should explain what changed in the team's understanding, how strong that change is, and what uncertainty still remains.

### Validated Learning
A learning that is strong enough to be treated as usable in synthesis and decision-making. In this playbook, validated learning is the bar for moving from raw evidence to confident next-step reasoning.

### Insight
A synthesis of one or more learnings that clarifies implications, trade-offs, or the meaning of the evidence for the broader bet.

### Decision
An explicit commitment based on insights. In this playbook, a decision should always point to action, ownership, and the next move. Common decision outcomes are `go`, `iterate`, `pivot`, or `stop`.

### Decision-ready
A state where the team has enough evidence, validated learning, and clarity on trade-offs to make a responsible next move. Decision-ready does not mean uncertainty is gone; it means uncertainty is low enough to act.

### MVP (Minimum Viable Product)
The smallest version of a product that allows you to test critical assumptions and learn from real users with minimal effort.

### Value Proposition
A clear statement of the unique value your solution provides to specific users in specific situations.

### Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD)
A framework for understanding what users are trying to accomplish: "When [context], I want to [goal], so that [outcome]."

## Process Terms

### Cadence
The operating rhythm teams use to keep learning cycles moving with consistent review and quality control. In this playbook, the standard pattern is weekly cycle planning, a mid-cycle evidence check, and an end-cycle decision review.

### Checkpoint
An execution point inside a learning cycle where the team should produce a specific output and reduce a specific kind of uncertainty. The five core checkpoints are assumption selection, hypothesis and experiment design, learning capture, insight synthesis, and decision.

### Stage Gate
A program-level readiness check used to keep quality visible across teams. Stage gates mark whether assumptions, experiments, learnings, or decisions are ready to move forward.

### Exit Criteria
Specific, measurable conditions that must be met before a phase, checkpoint, or gate is considered complete.

### Signal Criteria
The predefined condition, threshold, or result that tells the team what would count as meaningful evidence before running a test. Clear signal criteria protect teams from deciding after the fact what the results "meant."

### Inputs
Materials, information, or artifacts required before starting an activity.

### Artifacts
Tangible outputs produced during the workflow, such as assumptions, hypotheses, experiment records, learnings, insights, rubric scores, or decision notes.

### Traceability
The visible link from a learning or decision back to the experiment, hypothesis, and original assumption that produced it. Traceability keeps teams from re-litigating prior work or losing the logic behind a choice.

### Continuity
The degree to which work carries cleanly from one stage of the loop to the next without losing meaning, context, or ownership. Strong continuity reduces rework and repeated debate.

### Escalation
An intentional intervention when a team is stuck, repeatedly missing gates, or producing low-quality artifacts. Escalation exists to restore progress and signal quality early, not to punish teams.

### Role Lens
A perspective used to interpret the same workflow according to a user's needs. In this playbook, the most common role lenses are startup team, program manager, and mentor.

## Quality and Decision Terms

### Confidence
A practical judgment about whether the team has enough evidence to make the next responsible move. Confidence is not certainty; it is an explicit signal of how much uncertainty remains relative to the decision.

### Learning Velocity
The rate at which a team can move from uncertainty to usable learning. In SwiftCNS, learning velocity depends not only on running tests faster, but also on reducing loss, drift, and rework between stages.

### Rework
Repeat effort caused by weak earlier outputs, unclear definitions, or missing evidence. In this system, rework is usually a sign that a prior checkpoint or stage gate was too weak to support what came next.

### Experiment Theater
Work that looks active and serious but does not produce decision-relevant evidence. Experiment theater is a common failure mode because teams can stay busy while learning very little.

## Measurement Terms

### Median Learning Cycle Time
The primary north-star metric in this playbook: the median time from assumption selection to validated learning. The metric starts when a specific assumption is chosen for focused testing and ends when the resulting learning is strong enough to use in synthesis.

### Key Metric
A quantifiable measure used to evaluate success or progress toward an outcome.

### Leading Indicator
A metric that predicts future outcomes, such as early evidence that may affect later retention, adoption, or conversion.

### Lagging Indicator
A metric that reflects outcomes that have already happened, such as revenue or retention.

### Baseline
The current state before making changes, used for comparison.

### Cohort
A defined group of teams, users, or participants measured together over a shared time period or program experience. Cohort-level metrics help program leaders compare execution health across groups.

## Innovation Terms

### Thin Vertical
A narrow, complete user journey that demonstrates end-to-end value, even if limited in scope.

### Thin Horizontal
A broad feature set that covers many scenarios but may not be complete in any single journey.

### Iteration
A repeated cycle of learning and refinement. In the SwiftCNS context, iteration should be driven by better evidence and clearer decisions, not just by activity.

### Pivot
A fundamental change in strategy based on learning that the current approach will not achieve the desired outcome.

## Documentation Terms

### Template
A reusable structure or format for creating consistent artifacts.

### Worked Example
A complete, real-world example showing how to apply a concept or use a template.

### Checklist
A quick reference list of items to verify or complete.

### Self-Assessment
A reflective exercise or quiz to evaluate understanding or readiness.

### Learning Quality Rubric
A shared scoring tool for judging whether a learning is strong enough to support synthesis and decision-making. In this playbook, it evaluates evidence strength, traceability, clarity, confidence, and decision relevance.

### Decision Readiness Checklist
A final quality-control tool used before a decision review. It helps teams confirm that the highest-risk assumption was tested, the learning is validated, options are explicit, and ownership is clear.

## SwiftCNS Terms

### SwiftCNS
The learning system for innovation teams described in this playbook. SwiftCNS helps teams move through a repeatable loop of idea or problem, assumptions, experiments, learnings, insights, and decisions.

## Acronyms

- **PM**: Product Manager
- **Dev**: Developer
- **PRD**: Product Requirements Document
- **MVP**: Minimum Viable Product
- **JTBD**: Jobs-to-Be-Done
- **CNS**: Legacy acronym that appears in earlier materials. In this playbook, prefer the full product name `SwiftCNS`.

## Related Terms

Terms that appear together or are related:
- **Idea or Problem** -> **Assumption** -> **Critical Assumption** -> **Hypothesis** -> **Experiment**
- **Activity** -> **Observation** -> **Evidence** -> **Learning** -> **Validated Learning**
- **Validated Learning** -> **Insight** -> **Decision** -> **Next Assumption**
- **Checkpoint** -> **Exit Criteria** -> **Stage Gate** -> **Decision-ready**
- **Traceability** + **Continuity** -> stronger learning velocity, less rework
- **MVP** -> **Thin Vertical** -> **Iteration** -> **Pivot**
