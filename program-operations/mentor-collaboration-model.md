# Mentor Collaboration Model

**Purpose**: Integrate mentors into the cycle where they improve quality most.

Mentors are most valuable when they improve the quality of the cycle, not when they replace the team’s ownership of it.

## Mentor intervention points

- After assumption prioritization.
- Before experiment launch.
- During learning validation.
- Before final decision.

These moments matter because they are the points where weak reasoning tends to become expensive. A mentor who shows up at the right checkpoint can save a team days or weeks of avoidable drift.

## Review prompts for mentors

- Is the assumption truly critical?
- Is the hypothesis measurable and falsifiable?
- Does the experiment produce decision-relevant evidence?
- Are learnings evidence-backed and confidence-rated?
- Is the final decision explicit and accountable?

## What mentors should optimize for

The goal is not to make the team sound smarter. The goal is to make the team think more clearly and decide more responsibly.

That usually means mentors should:

- ask sharper questions instead of giving immediate answers,
- push for clearer assumptions and cleaner evidence,
- identify where confidence is being overstated,
- help the team see trade-offs before a decision is made.

## Team expectations

- Share artifacts before review sessions.
- Capture decisions and action items immediately.
- Track feedback adoption in the next cycle.

## What good collaboration looks like

Good mentor collaboration is timely, specific, and grounded in artifacts.

It should feel like:

- the team stays accountable for the work,
- the mentor improves the quality of judgment,
- the conversation is anchored in real outputs,
- and the result is a clearer next move.

## Common failure mode

The usual failure here is one of two extremes:

- mentors are brought in too late, after weak work has already compounded,
- or mentors become so involved that the team starts outsourcing judgment.

The healthiest model is somewhere in the middle: high-leverage intervention, low ownership displacement.
