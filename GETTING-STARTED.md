# Start Here

**Purpose**: Get your team operational in SwiftCNS and start a high-quality learning cycle quickly.

## Outcome

By the end of this section, your team is ready to run the full SwiftCNS learning loop:
**assumptions -> experiments -> learnings -> insights -> decisions**.

More importantly, your team should understand *why* the path is structured this way and what good execution looks like before real work begins.

## Time to complete

30-45 minutes.

## Who this is for

- Startup teams validating new products or bets.
- Program managers running cohorts across multiple teams.
- Mentors supporting teams with structured feedback.

## Prerequisites

- A SwiftCNS account.
- A project idea, initiative, or problem area.
- At least one teammate or mentor available for review.

## Why this section exists

Many teams want to jump straight into experiments. The problem is that execution without shared framing usually creates noise instead of learning. Teams move fast, but they are not aligned on:

what they are actually trying to learn, how to define quality, what counts as evidence, and when a decision is strong enough to make.

This section exists to prevent that. It gives teams a fast but structured starting point so they do not spend the next several cycles correcting avoidable confusion.

## One canonical path

Use this sequence to maximize SwiftCNS usage:

1. Complete [30-Minute Activation](start-here/30-minute-activation.md).
2. Read [Concept Foundations](concept-foundations/README.md).
3. Learn [Learning Cycle Fundamentals](learning-cycle/fundamentals/README.md).
4. Execute [Run Learning Cycles in SwiftCNS](run-learning-cycles/README.md).

## Why the path is ordered this way

This path is deliberate.

### 1. Activation comes first

Before anything else, teams need a live project, an initial problem frame, and a working starting point. Without that, concept pages stay abstract.

### 2. Concepts come before execution

Teams need shared definitions before they start reviewing artifacts together. If one person thinks an assumption is the same as a hypothesis, and another person treats an experiment like a task list, cycle quality drops immediately.

### 3. Fundamentals come before workflows

Before running the workflow, teams need to understand the anatomy of a learning cycle, what quality looks like, and how failure patterns show up in practice.

### 4. Execution comes before expansion

The first priority is learning the core cycle well. Extra operating layers can come later, once the team has real cycle habits and real evidence to work from.

## What success looks like at the end of onboarding

At the end of this onboarding layer, a strong team should be able to explain the SwiftCNS loop in plain language, distinguish assumptions, hypotheses, experiments, learnings, insights, and decisions, identify where they are in the cycle at any given moment, understand what outputs are needed before moving to the next stage, and begin execution with aligned expectations across operators, reviewers, and decision makers.

This is the difference between merely having access to a tool and actually adopting a system.

## What teams often get wrong early

The most common early mistakes are:

- starting with too many assumptions at once,
- treating activity as if it were evidence,
- skipping explicit decision criteria,
- moving to synthesis before learnings are strong enough,
- expecting mentors or program managers to correct issues that should have been addressed in the workflow itself.

If your team recognizes these patterns early, it can save weeks of confusion later.

## Definition of done

You are ready for execution when:

- You completed activation.
- You understand core concepts and quality standards.
- You can identify where your team is in the learning cycle.
- You are ready to begin [01 — Idea / Problem](run-learning-cycles/01-idea-problem.md).

## Practical advice before you move on

Start narrower than feels comfortable. Pick one meaningful assumption before picking five. Favor short cycles over perfect plans. Use the concept pages to improve judgment, not just vocabulary. And treat the first cycle as a calibration cycle for your team, not just for the idea.
